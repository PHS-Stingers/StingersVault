| **Mechanical** | Set realistic goals<br>Don't spend the entire lead up (10-14 days) to competitions and even competition days working towards the end product of what your robot could be. It's easy to get caught up in knowing the potential you have and getting sucked into a high stress, high pressure situation where, let's be honest, not everyone can produce their best.<br><br>Instead spend your time working with what you have as the main focus, this means practicing driving, practicing your presentation for the judges, planning for partners with different capabilities with the current state of your robot in mind. <br>Work to be able to compete with what you have. Sometimes a push-bot, if used really well, can be the deciding factor in a match.<br>I'm not saying don't work to improve, I'm saying don't bank on the improvement that may not work out and think that everything else will come later.                                                   | Something great our coach tries very had to instill in us, that we somehow still mess up, is the philosophy: <br>"Do the simple thing first."<br>In a world as multi-faceted and fast-paced as FTC, it's so common that one will have a great idea that is planned for future, an idea they put down as "for after the intake is done" or "when the main code is done, I'll knock this out in  an hour".<br>Things tend to pile up QUICKLY when you work like this. <br>Then you get to the night before and you're cramming all those "one hour" tasks into the 12 hours before the competition, not to mention finding time to sleep <br>(we cannot reiterate this enough, PLEASE SLEEP) <br>"We should suggest FIRST introduce ranking points for actually sleeping"<br><br>Do the simple thing first.<br>Stuff gets overwhelming, especially close to competition time. Putting in a little time and effort to work out something that's gonna save you a bunch of time lessens your load which makes everything easier to deal with and also gives you more time at crunch-time to work on the big things or just get some R&R.<br> |     |

| **Game**       | Know the rules:<br>specifically the sections on matches, the field and one's personal robot.<br>These are the sections that define what you can and cannot do. Knowing these sections back-to-front gives the advantage of knowing what situations one can capitalise on foul points and also knowing how to avoid falling (or being pushed) into those same traps.<br>Especially in seasons with intense fouling and referee work like Decode had, foul points very quickly become a major deciding factor in matches.<br><br>There were more than a few times last season where we saw teams go from a major lead at the end of a game to barely winning or even losing. <br>"This knocked us down a solid couple of positions last season" <br>"thats only because _someone_ insisted they had to drive, despite getting 3 hours of sleep and drove into the red alliance's ~~release lever~~..."<br>Next rule, get some sleep. Energy drinks and coffee doesn't count" | Have your co-pilot/advisor watching the entire field.<br>Have them watch other matches and take notes so they can learn the ins and outs, get a feel for what it's like to not only watch a match, but analyze one. <br>Being able to keep up with all four robots and quickly analyze the field are SUCH useful skills.<br>It allows the advisor to help the drivers avoid situations that could lead to fouls and to be aware of fouls committed by other teams.<br><br>Driver(s) MUST listen to the advisor though. Yes, there will be moments where they have an instinct to do something else that pays off but overall let the drivers focus on driving and have their advisor watching everything else. It's super easy to get overwhelmed as a driver who has to do it all.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |     |
